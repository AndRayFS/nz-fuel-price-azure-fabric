Which layers the model touches

The model does not forecast oil, and it does not forecast tax. It takes the cost movement that has already happened and works out how much of it has not reached the pump yet.

Arithmetic

How to read the right-hand column. It is the average size of each layer's weekly change, in cents per litre, shown as a share of all the movement in that period. It measures how much a layer stirs, not whether it pushed the price up or down.

Refining and shipping are shown together. They can only be separated for weeks where an Argus quote is available through the Australian Institute of Petroleum, and those start in late 2025. Where both are known, shipping and wharfage account for roughly 8–13 c/L of that band.

The 2026 weeks are still provisional. MBIE suspended its cost and margin series in March, backfilled them, and the rows stay provisional until the quarter's CPI is published. Every crisis figure here will be recomputed when they finalise.

“NZ chain + margin” is a residual, not a measurement. It is what remains after tax and landed cost are subtracted, so every error in the cost model lands in it with the sign flipped. It is a gross margin for the whole domestic chain — not any company's profit.
